# Representative visual sample

This is a production dependency, not a quota of assets, screenshots or helper tools.
On a fresh run or continuation, inspect the actual project before expanding content.
If the game cannot render or accept input, repair that prerequisite first. Respect
explicit source-only/testing restrictions and leave visual evidence pending.

## Work order

1. Choose the intended mood and concrete visual references, silhouettes/proportions,
   palette, material treatment, scene density, camera composition and UI scale. Write
   the decisions in the existing plan's ART item, with links to longer art notes.
2. Audit four resource roles: player, companion, environment and interface. Record
   what is merely downloaded, what is imported and what is visibly instantiated.
   Choose compatible assets or deliberately authored art for the important subjects.
   Trees/rocks alone do not replace a primitive cast or unfinished buildings. Inspect
   available catalog alternatives if the first provider or format fails. Never require
   a specific vendor, network download, or paid pack when suitable local art exists.
3. Build one arrival scene with depth, recognizable animated actors, believable ground,
   water/shore boundaries, a readable path and a focal point. Fix material scale,
   repetition, exposed blockout and lighting before expanding maps or dialogue volume.
4. Play at the intended resolution. Inspect arrival, dialogue and battle frames, plus
   movement, camera changes and one battle action. Compare equivalent before/after
   views. Record specific defects and change the art; repeating a screenshot or a
   brightness/layout metric is not another useful iteration.
5. If the sample fails, its next concrete art fix takes priority over new quests/maps,
   broad unrelated regression and release packaging. Run affected checks as needed.
   A stable partial preview is allowed when useful, labelled with unresolved art.
   Finish gameplay and other acceptance journeys after the visual foundation exists.

Evaluate these criteria independently:

- `composition`: focal point, foreground/midground/background, clear route, intentional
  density/variation and camera visibility. Rows of identical props are not composition.
- `characters`: recognizable player and companion silhouettes, consistent proportions,
  expressive faces/poses and no unresolved primitive stand-ins for important actors.
- `materials_lighting`: coherent palette/materials, legible depth and ground/water,
  controlled highlights/shadows; no washed-out flat scene or incompatible asset styles.
- `ui`: legible at the target resolution without overlapping world labels or obscuring
  the action. Use a dedicated battle/dialogue layout rather than giant empty modals.
- `motion`: observe movement/turning and a battle action. Evaluate foot contact, pose
  transitions and visible action feedback; a still image or animation import is not proof.

## Keep the review in the existing plan

Use the existing ART item in plan.md for the chosen direction, resources visibly used,
actual observations, unresolved defects and the next concrete improvement. Keep only
its current status and next action in the Brief. Link screenshots or longer notes when
useful; no separate JSON schema, source-fingerprint script or mandatory report is required.

The same GameBuilder performs the review. Look at native screenshots and actual motion,
judge them against the task's intended experience, then improve the relevant game assets,
scene or UI. Do not treat a helper score, model count or completed checklist as a substitute
for looking at the result. Describe concrete evidence rather than simply declaring it pretty.

On continuation or after a relevant change, inspect the current game again. Earlier
screenshots and recorded opinions are historical until you confirm they still apply.
Finish a coherent implementation batch and replay affected views; do not restart every
art step after each small code edit. Preserve unaffected work and prioritize visible defects.

At handoff, distinguish your visual judgment, observed gameplay, remaining work and
agent-system evolution. Report unfinished art honestly. These instructions guide the
agent's own work; there is no runtime aesthetic judge or additional completion gate.
For source-only work, respect the user's execution limits and leave visual review pending.
