Project brief · Research workbench · From factors to strategies

Signal Foundry

Turn a stock's price and volume history into explainable factors, testable trading hypotheses and a beautiful visual report whose conclusions can be inspected.

Mission and research scope

Create a research system with an attractive visual report for a stock, discovering useful factors and jointly improving them with testable trading strategies across diverse research hypotheses. Factor discovery remains open throughout strategy research. The two areas form one continuous report page, with clear conclusions and evidence that readers can explore by scrolling, without switching pages or tabs between the stages.

The accompanying study.json specifies the stock, dates, trading assumptions, research quality requirements and evaluation scope. The default study uses NVDA daily OHLCV. Detailed implementation choices remain open.

The user must be able to answer: What did we download? Why might this factor work? Which evidence admitted or rejected it? How did it change a strategy? What survives costs, another market regime and the untouched final evaluation?

A trustworthy data foundation

Base the research on real, lawfully accessible stock prices and trading volume, with the corporate actions and trading-session information needed to interpret them. Readers should be able to identify the source, actual coverage and adjustment assumptions, and understand any missing data or access limitation. This study researches one stock; a multi-stock selection product is outside its scope.

Retain raw prices and volume, split and dividend records, provider/feed identity, retrieval time, dataset hashes and a data-quality report. Detect duplicate bars, missing sessions, invalid OHLC ranges and inconsistent adjustments. A single-venue volume feed, fabricated data or a short convenience sample cannot silently replace the requested study.

Use lawfully accessible public historical data to complete the research. If verified as-traded prices or consolidated volume are unavailable, the study permits a documented adjusted-price research proxy. Preserve provider fields, adjustment rules and limitations. Show strict data qualification separately as unmet where necessary; this does not block factor evaluation, strategy research or numerical reports on the declared public-data basis. Never present proxy fills as verified historical executions or weaken the declared evidence standard.

Distinguish measured data quality from assumptions. Unavailable history must remain visible as a limitation rather than being replaced by fabricated observations.

Factor discovery and the Factor Observatory

Design strategy hypotheses and the factors needed by each hypothesis together. Evaluate factors for their declared roles and their contribution to those strategies, while retaining a reusable versioned factor library. Each conclusion must come from reproducible calculations on the specified observations.

Explore distinct economic hypotheses, invent new ones from observed evidence, and revise promising factors when strategy research exposes a weakness or opportunity. Trend persistence, shock recovery and conditional risk are examples, not a fixed catalog. Investigate different mechanisms and uses rather than only variations of one formula. Give every candidate an ID, formula, units, lookback, availability time, prediction horizon, intended role, direction where applicable, rationale and falsification condition. Parameter variations count as trials, not new mechanisms. Select directions and transformations on training observations only.

Show every proposed and evaluated factor in the report's factor section, distinguishing pending, blocked and failed evaluations from admitted or rejected research candidates. Readers must see which factors were mined, their formulas and how well each performed. Include a sortable candidate table, time-series IC and RankIC for return-prediction hypotheses, role-appropriate measurements for other uses, and by-horizon/fold results, rolling stability, conditional response curves, coverage and missingness, uncertainty, regime behavior, redundancy clusters and admission reasons. Define metrics before use. A single stock has no daily cross-sectional IC. Show insufficient evidence explicitly instead of inventing confidence or passing a factor.

The factor library grows with the research. Preserve expressions, parent versions, fitted state, code/data versions and role-specific qualification evidence. Each strategy chooses its own relevant factor versions; sharing is possible but not compulsory. Show which strategies benefited from a factor revision and which did not. Qualification for one use does not imply suitability for every strategy. Rejected versions cannot reappear as eligible simply because a strategy needs them.

Strategy mining and the Strategy Atelier

Construct executable strategy hypotheses with their required factors, and evaluate their returns, risks, costs and factor contributions in the same research round. Readers should be able to trace a strategy back to its factor evidence and understand how signals lead to positions, trades and profit or loss.

Evaluate suitable single-factor controls alongside multi-factor hypotheses to explain each factor's contribution. Explore multiple distinct trading hypotheses, with their own relevant factors and signal-to-position logic. A common factor set, renamed policy or different position size does not by itself demonstrate diverse strategy discovery. Specify entry, exit, sizing, rebalance timing, neutral states and regime rules. Use the declared long-or-cash execution model. Measure the incremental value of each factor with ablations and compare against cash and holding the same stock. Rank candidates using the frozen validation objective, risk and complexity, not the prettiest training curve.

List all strategy candidates with their exact factor composition, readable trading rules, actual evaluation state, results and selection/rejection reasons. Show labelled train, validation and final-test sections together on the same page, with gross/net equity, benchmark comparison, drawdown and recovery, monthly/yearly returns, exposure, turnover, transaction costs, trade distributions and traceable orders. Report CAGR, annualized volatility, Sharpe, Sortino, Calmar, maximum drawdown, trade count, win rate, payoff/profit factor and cost sensitivity with definitions and null states. Include fold/regime results, parameter stability, uncertainty, factor ablations and all unsuccessful candidates.

Research loop and the final evaluation

Start with roughly ten diverse strategy hypotheses and each strategy's required factors, then evaluate them together on train and validation. Select a working candidate pool using measured effects, stability, costs, behavioral diversity and specific improvement potential. No fixed number of survivors is required. For every retained strategy, diagnose whether to revise its factors, trading rules or their interaction; measure useful changes or explain why further work on that route is not worthwhile.

Subsequent rounds combine candidate improvements with new strategy hypotheses and needed factors. Aim for about 100 evaluated factor definitions and 20–30 distinct strategy hypotheses across the research, as specified by study.json. These are cumulative scope references, not minimum candidate counts, prerequisites for starting strategy evaluation, admission quotas or search ceilings. Adapt to evidence and explain material shortfalls. Renaming, parameter tuning and benchmarks do not establish new mechanisms. Preserve failed candidates, exact versions and individual before/after evidence.

Retain machine-readable results and decisions for every round, with stable candidate, version, evaluation and round identities. A discoverable index links factor definitions, strategy bindings, metrics, comparisons, pool decisions and the next proposed experiments. Keep completed historical records intact. The visual report displays these same results.

Keep training, validation and test results distinct. Selection must use only information available at the relevant time, with no future-data leakage. Fix the evaluation criteria before research and finalize the selected factors and strategy before the final test. Both report sections should describe that same final evaluation; test factor rankings must not influence a strategy that is still being optimized.

The researcher decides when to continue or stop. Continue when a concrete next experiment has worthwhile expected information or improvement value. Completion may follow supported research objectives, or repeated ineffective improvements and low expected value from more work. No fixed return target, candidate count, patience counter or exhaustive search is required. Before final evaluation, justify the selected candidate through its mechanism, factor contributions, robustness, costs and uncertainty against prospective criteria.

Complete the research only with a defensible assessment supported by reproducible evidence: clear mechanisms, diverse investigated alternatives, individual candidate reviews, fold/regime and parameter stability, realistic execution costs, adequate sample support, explicit search effects and an honest interpretation of final-test results. Address counterevidence rather than relying on one attractive statistic. A supported negative or inconclusive finding may close the investigation; explain the scope tested and why further work is not worthwhile, without claiming all useful strategies have been ruled out. If no candidate qualifies, preserve the unexposed test and state why it was not run. Completed research and a supported trading strategy are different outcomes.

A failed final result may lead to further exploratory work on training and validation. Preserve the frozen result: repeatedly tuning on the exposed test cannot produce fresh confirmation. New confirmatory claims require genuinely unused observations under a prospectively fixed protocol. If time, resources or access prevent useful progress, label the research interrupted or blocked and retain unfinished questions. Each experiment starts independently; its trial history and test exposure remain immutable within it.

A research workbench worth exploring

Design a premium analytical interface: disciplined typography, calm surfaces, deliberate spacing, deep-green backgrounds, layered green surfaces, mint emphasis, light text, amber uncertainty and red losses. Use a coherent visual direction and an investigative layout rather than interchangeable KPI cards.

Use one continuous page for the overview, factor inventory and evidence, strategy inventory and evidence, research decisions and metric definitions. A table of contents may scroll to sections; it must not replace them with another page or a hidden tab. Local sorting, filtering and chart comparisons should preserve the surrounding investigation. All factor and strategy summary rows should be available by default.

  • A study overview connects source provenance, the chronological split timeline, research progress and separate engineering/research acceptance states.
  • A Factor Observatory lets a visitor filter candidates, compare horizons and folds, inspect a formula and trace selection into a strategy.
  • A Strategy Atelier links factor composition, ablations, equity/drawdown charts, trade markers, fold comparisons and a readable, downloadable trade ledger.
  • Aligned charts and saved trade/exposure tables explain major drawdowns. Tooltips or optional descriptive filters may aid reading without changing official scores.
  • Cost-sensitivity charts compare predeclared, computed scenarios; displayed controls cannot overwrite the accepted result or initiate test optimization.
  • An experiment lineage view links hypotheses, rejected trials, versions and artifacts. Show research routes, strategy-specific factor bindings and before/after evidence for factor and strategy refinements, including each shortlisted route's next step or closure. Visitors can download the permitted metric tables, a compact structured analysis summary and a reproducibility manifest.
  • Every line or bar chart states its question, axes, units, series, time range, aggregation and sample support. Show baselines, uncertainty where justified, missing values and a readable conclusion. Definitions, charts, tables and downloads must agree.
  • The final-test panel stays unavailable until the final evaluation. Test values must not be accessible through other report views or downloads beforehand.

Provide readable charts, a responsive layout and useful loading, pending and failed states. The report is a visualization of saved JSON results: HTML supplies the page structure and JavaScript loads the data for display. Research analysis consumes the JSON directly and does not depend on browser interaction or reading HTML. Publish a useful integrated snapshot and retain prior report versions; report releases are not research iterations.

Delivery and acceptance evidence

  • A working joint factor/strategy research workflow that can evaluate new hypotheses and revisions on the specified stock and reproduce its conclusions.
  • Real data provenance, frozen protocol, factor library, strategy specifications, trial history, deterministic checks and reproducible JSON metrics, with a discoverable versioned index and per-round selection decisions.
  • One detailed, continuous report page showing which factors and strategies were explored, what their definitions are and how they performed, including rejected candidates. Both stages remain readable without changing pages or tabs. Displayed metrics and chart values reconcile with exported records, with usable scroll navigation and interactions.
  • Explicit research decision and evidence for every quality dimension, supported or unsupported strategy claims, test exposure, counterevidence, remaining investigations, limitations and reproduction instructions. Distinguish completed research from resource interruption.

This is historical research software. Do not place orders, promise future profit, purchase data subscriptions or claim independent evaluation from self-review.
