Pulse City

Create an explorable civic simulation in which residents can understand a living neighborhood, experience competing needs, propose interventions, and see plausible consequences over time. The product should make public systems tangible and invite meaningful authorship, not imitate a municipal portal, reduce civic life to opinion polling, or pretend complex trade-offs have one mathematically perfect answer.

Core journey

A visitor adopts a resident perspective, explores places and stories, detects a pressure or opportunity, examines evidence, and tries a bounded intervention. The city responds across more than one dimension—such as access, cost, time, safety, ecology, belonging, or trust—and explains the connection between action and outcome. Visitors can compare, revise, or replay a plan before contributing it to a public design process.

City systems

  • Use a coherent neighborhood, realistic residents, places, constraints, and time horizon.
  • Connect spatial exploration, human stories, evidence, proposals, and consequences.
  • Reveal uncertainty and distributional effects instead of only a single aggregate score.
  • Preserve a visitor’s saved lens, route, plans, comparisons, and contribution history.
  • Provide recovery from dead ends and explain which outcomes are simulated assumptions.

Resident-led development

Treat residents as co-designers of both the simulated city and the website experience. They may request a new neighborhood feature, policy experiment, representation, data explanation, accessibility mode, deliberation mechanism, or way of comparing futures. Let them distinguish a personal viewing/interaction preference from a shared civic proposal, state affected people and non-negotiables, and define an acceptance scenario. Delivered proposals need visible, immutable provenance and a comprehensible public status without leaking private profiles.

Ethical and interaction quality

Avoid surveillance, partisan persuasion, false precision, dark patterns, and claims that the simulation predicts reality. Do not let majority preference erase a minority’s safety or access need. Support keyboard use, readable and reduced-motion modes, narrow screens, plain language, transparent assumptions, and alternative representations for spatial or visual information. Choose any suitable implementation; favor one deep, polished district over an empty city-scale shell.

Scenario success

Success means different residents can pursue different civic questions, understand a real trade-off, preserve their own lens, author a proposal, and return to a later version that can demonstrate how their participation changed either their personal experience or the shared city. Only observed reusable gaps may trigger framework self-evolution.
