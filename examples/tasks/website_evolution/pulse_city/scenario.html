Pulse City — A neighbourhood of possible futures

A little city you want to spend time in

Build a fancy, genuinely interactive browser experience about everyday life in a fictional neighbourhood. Aim for the visual finish and satisfying feedback of a polished small simulation game. The pleasure is noticing how a place works, trying a change, and discovering an unexpected consequence for its people. This is not a municipal portal, policy spreadsheet, opinion poll or dashboard around a decorative map.

Make places the main experience. Choose your own visual language: animated illustration, an isometric model, a tactile map or lightweight 3D can all work. Streets must respond visibly to exploration and intervention, not merely swap numbers in a side panel. No developer chat, feedback inbox or experiment controls belongs in the product. Conversations about improving the website happen outside it.

Notice, try, watch, reconsider

Let a visitor enter a coherent district, follow a person or investigate a place, discover a pressure or opportunity, and try a bounded change. Advance a clearly labelled simulation period and show its effects on places and daily life. Compare with the starting state, undo, and try an alternative. This complete loop must work without an account, a manual or an LLM request for every action.

Connect enough places for real choices: homes, work, a shared destination and routes between them, for example. Daily rhythms should make the district feel inhabited. A changed walking route, shopfront or resident reaction can make consequences tangible; these are possibilities, not a checklist. Make the first experiment understandable within minutes, with a reason to try another.

Offer a small set of interventions with distinct trade-offs, not cosmetic recolours. Context should matter: a change can improve access while shifting cost, noise or space elsewhere. Choose the systems yourself. Do not force one correct policy or make every trade-off a punishment; leave room for constructive combinations and discovery.

A legible model, not a prediction of reality

Use consistent fictional geography, people, resources and time scales. Explain assumptions near the outcome. Separate starting conditions, choices and authored or calculated effects. Label scripted outcomes honestly. If chance matters, allow comparisons from comparable starting conditions. Invented statistics are not real local evidence.

Show who gains, who faces a cost, and what remains unknown rather than compressing everything into a happiness score. Residents have agency and more than one concern. Avoid caricatures, surveillance, political persuasion or treating minority access and safety as disposable points. This is fictional exploration, not civic advice.

One finished district, many possible stories

First deliver one compact, richly composed district and a complete explore-change-compare loop. A few meaningful interventions beat dozens of empty buildings. Expansion, new perspectives, cooperative planning and richer simulation are later possibilities, not prerequisites for the first usable release.

Make the opening worth looking at: deliberate type and palette, recognizable landmarks, varied street detail, readable scale and purposeful motion. Changes should feel placed in the world, with observable before/after differences. Avoid default component styling, a wall of instructions or animation that makes inspection slow.

Support keyboard use, visible focus, narrow screens and reduced motion. Provide another way to understand places and consequences without relying only on colour, dragging or tiny moving objects. Preserve the rich main view while offering alternatives. Sound is optional and starts off; expensive effects should degrade gracefully.

Return with a different question

Preserve the visitor's experiment and comparisons in the same browser. Make the active scenario clear, support undo and confirmed reset, and protect saved work across updates. Handle unavailable storage honestly. Local experiments do not imply a shared city, cross-device accounts, public voting or delivery to real authorities.

Visitors may enjoy wandering, understanding a mechanism or telling a story. Let use and conversation shape later changes to discovery, explanation or participation. Separate personal viewing preferences from world state so accommodating one person does not silently replace another's experience. No predefined persona modes, proposal approval workflow or fixed expansion roadmap is required.

Prove that the city responds

On the exact deployed version, use ordinary controls to explore, inspect a need, apply a change, observe consequences, compare an alternative, undo and reload saved work. Check a keyboard route and phone layout. If a prerequisite fails, record it and mark dependent checks blocked rather than continuing a meaningless checklist.

Working controls alone do not prove a compelling simulation. Show a visible spatial change, an understandable human consequence and a reason to reconsider a choice. Compare equivalent starting conditions and views across revisions. Distinguish a bug fix from a new decision or visual redesign; state model limitations and untested paths honestly.
