ORBITAL — A living universe

Feel the universe's scale and understand its motion

Build a magnificent, deeply interactive three-dimensional universe observatory. Visitors explore many galaxies, enter a richly realized Milky Way, find our Solar System within it and descend to planetary detail. They can also observe the Solar System travelling through galactic space while planets orbit the Sun. The experience should inspire the wonder of a great planetarium and give visitors the agency to investigate what they see.

This connected cosmic journey is the scope of the first usable release. Develop a complete, visually accomplished route across these scales, with meaningful opportunities to explore alternatives. A small orbital diagram, an isolated physics lab or a cinematic opening that leads to plain information cards does not satisfy the product. The purpose is exploration and scientific understanding, without missions, resources or progression gates.

Many galaxies, a Milky Way you can enter, a Solar System you can find

Make many galaxies visible as distinct systems with varied morphology, orientation and spatial depth. Let visitors select, approach and inspect multiple galaxies. Choose a substantial but feasible collection; there is no arbitrary object quota. A gallery of interchangeable spiral icons or a star field with renamed points cannot convey this scope. Additional procedural populations can enrich the scene when their illustrative status is clear.

The Milky Way deserves particular depth: recognizable spiral structure, a central concentration, a stellar disk with thickness and a surrounding halo. Show how its appearance changes from above, from the side and within the disk. Locate the Solar System using sourced astronomical context. Make the journey from galaxy to solar neighbourhood to planets legible, with spatial continuity, useful orientation and a reliable way back.

Planetary views should reward close inspection with three-dimensional form, directional sunlight, distinctive surfaces and appropriate atmospheric or ring detail. Preserve the relationship between these local objects and their larger setting. Choose how selection, navigation and level of detail work; the user should feel they are exploring a connected universe rather than switching between unrelated screens.

Follow the Solar System through space

Represent planetary orbital motion together with the Solar System's motion through the Milky Way. Let visitors compare a Sun-centred view with a galactic or clearly defined local inertial view. They should be able to inspect the resulting winding trajectories from different angles, pause at a meaningful moment and understand what each frame shows.

Generate trajectories from the composed model, using consistent positions, times and coordinate transformations. Planetary orbits remain approximately coplanar with their actual inclinations; account for the orientation of their plane relative to the Galaxy and to solar motion. Do not assign arbitrary tilts or draw a perfect decorative helix. Winding paths depend on the chosen frame and interval; the Sun does not mechanically drag a line of trailing planets.

Handle the enormous difference between planetary and galactic time scales deliberately. Display the active time scale, trail interval, units and relevant scale exaggerations. Keep a coherent physical clock in views presented as physical comparisons. A separately accelerated explanatory view can make a relationship easier to see, but its transformations must be clear. Changing the camera or reference frame should preserve the same physical scenario.

Give curiosity somewhere to go

Support a complete explore-inspect-question-compare journey. Visitors can change their viewpoint and time, focus on interesting objects, inspect a relationship and return to a known state. Design a few meaningful ways to manipulate or compare the model, so the experience goes beyond orbiting a camera and opening fact panels.

Choose interactions that strengthen the cosmic journey. Comparing reference frames, tracing a body's history, changing a physical condition in an explicitly hypothetical experiment, relating observations to a model, or following a personally chosen discovery route are possibilities. These are inspiration, not a mandatory collection of panels. Distinguish a visitor's experiment from the reference universe and offer understandable reset.

Show the consequence of a changed input through the model and an appropriate visible explanation or measurement. Let visitors repeat a comparison from equivalent conditions. Explanations should help someone answer a question about what they just did, while leaving the main scene available for further exploration.

Realism you can explain

Realism means convincing spatial structure, physically grounded motion and traceable information. Use suitable astronomical data, models and imagery, with sources and relevant reference epochs. Distinguish measured catalogue entries, reconstructed structure, statistically generated populations and numerical results. A named real galaxy's appearance and location should not be invented and presented as observation.

Choose an appropriate physical model at each scale and explain how the levels connect. Approximate galactic potentials, analytic planetary orbits and numerical experiments can coexist when their assumptions are explicit. A browser need not integrate every star in every galaxy. Do not claim a full cosmological simulation, live ephemerides or verified precision beyond what is implemented. Galactic motion must use an explained model; avoid silently treating the central black hole as the Galaxy's entire gravitating mass.

Make compression of distance, enlargement of bodies and other illustrative transformations understandable at the point of use. Keep readouts, trails and labels consistent with them. Validate representative motion, frame transformations and time-step sensitivity where relevant. Handle invalid inputs, lost objects and numerical failure with explicit recovery.

A universe worth looking into

The principal experience must be genuinely three-dimensional: inspectable volume, depth, parallax, spatially coherent motion and detailed near views. Choose the engine and rendering techniques yourself. Efficient particles, billboards, instancing or procedural detail are welcome inside a coherent 3D world; rotating a flat illustration alone is insufficient.

Use a distinctive visual identity with carefully composed light, stellar density, dust, materials, typography and annotation. Let galaxies have character and planets have recognizable detail. Make scale transitions beautiful and useful, with precise selection feedback and a restrained interface that keeps the scene prominent. Visual quality must hold up in ordinary exploration, including side views and close-ups, not only in one staged opening image.

Keep navigation responsive as complexity grows. Choose a sensible approach to loading, coordinates and detail levels. Offer appropriate quality controls and honest rendering-failure states. Support keyboard and touch interaction, visible focus, readable contrast, a considered phone layout and reduced camera motion. Visitors must be able to pause and think. Optional sound starts off; essential meaning remains available without it.

Carry a discovery beyond this visit

Choose a useful way to save and revisit an investigation: a reconstructable shared view, an experiment record, an annotated route or another artifact appropriate to the experience. Preserve the meaningful choices and model settings behind it. Implement the promised reload, export or reconstruction behaviour, identify model versions and handle older state explicitly.

Let different visitors enjoy wandering, investigating or explaining. Actual feedback may reshape a journey, a visualization or the model's explanatory layer. A preference for calm observation should coexist with deeper exploration. Improved colours alone do not resolve a user's difficulty understanding motion or finding their place in the universe.

Let discovery create new possibilities

New ideas may involve astronomical catalogues, imagery, recorded observations, media, comparative experiments or a form of exploration not anticipated here. Public APIs and unfamiliar data formats are possible ingredients when they serve a concrete visitor need. No particular provider, dataset or optional phenomenon is required.

Investigate worthwhile ideas through relevant documentation and a small feasible experiment. Preserve the essential user value when choosing a bounded implementation. If access or evidence is unavailable, explain the dependency instead of pretending the integration works. The initial design and the currently available tools are not a ceiling on imagination.

Strong experience goals, open design choices

The broad galaxy field, explorable Milky Way, connected planetary detail, genuine 3D presentation and explained combined motion are requirements. The collection's exact size, additional phenomena, interaction grammar, interface, branding and architecture are yours to design. Optional examples are invitations, not a feature checklist.

Complete a polished route through the required scales first, then let observed use shape further depth. Do not defer the cosmic scope to a hypothetical later version. Users may propose surprising experiences without prescribing implementation, and the Builder may rethink its initial approach to serve them. No fixed roadmap, persona modes or quota of new requests is prescribed.

Keep product-development dialogue outside the observatory. Explain what the delivered product actually does and distinguish observed evidence from intention. Source hashes and repeated deployments alone do not establish an improved visitor experience.

Travel through the universe you built

On the exact deployed version, use ordinary controls to inspect multiple galaxies, enter the Milky Way, locate the Solar System, approach a planet and return. Inspect galactic depth from face-on and edge-on viewpoints, and verify that planetary close-ups contain real detail. Capture representative views along this journey and explain what preserves orientation.

At equivalent physical conditions, switch reference frames and inspect combined-motion trails. Check their sampled positions against the model and explain the displayed time and distance scales. Try a meaningful model comparison and the offered save/reconstruction flow. Check keyboard use, a narrow screen, reduced motion and important recovery paths. Choose concrete acceptance steps to fit your design rather than inventing an unrelated checklist.

Report visual, interactive and scientific evidence separately. A technically functioning selector does not establish a convincing galaxy; impressive animation does not validate physics. After feedback, repeat the affected journey and show an observable change. Record blocked prerequisites and untested claims honestly.

Starting points for research

Consult NASA Galaxies, NASA Solar System facts and NASA Basics of Space Flight: the Solar System, then inspect the specific data, coordinate conventions and physical-model documentation your design needs. Overview pages are useful starting points; they do not validate a numerical implementation or establish asset usage rights.
