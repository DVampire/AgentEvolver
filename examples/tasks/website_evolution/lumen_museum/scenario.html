LUMEN — Museum of AI & Technology

A museum that makes an idea click

Create a beautiful and genuinely educational online museum about AI, computing or related technology. Choose a coherent curatorial angle and an identity that makes the subject compelling. A visitor should leave understanding something they could not explain before, grounded in an interaction they performed.

Aim for the wonder of a great science museum and the usefulness of a clear explanation. Put a working, intriguing exhibit near the opening. Let visitors pursue their curiosity without requiring fictional collectibles or solving unrelated puzzles to reach the learning.

Visitors experiment, not just reveal more text

Design a small, coherent exhibition with meaningful variation in what visitors can investigate. Each central exhibit should connect a question, an intentional action, an observable result and an explanation. Give visitors a way to change an input or condition and understand why the outcome changes. Choose the number, subjects and interaction forms yourself.

Possible inspirations include learning from examples, seeing how a network transforms information, exploring attention, comparing search and generation, manipulating a simple circuit, following a computing invention or discovering how a visual system fails. These are alternatives and prompts for imagination, not a required curriculum. An original direction is welcome if it teaches a real concept through interaction.

Give the visitor agency to compare, undo, reset or investigate another possibility using a form appropriate to the exhibit. Make the mechanism visible enough to learn from: a visual transformation, a manipulable model, a causal diagram or a measured experiment can all work. Decorative motion and input-insensitive results do not establish educational interaction.

Wonder with intellectual substance

Connect exhibits through worthwhile context: what problem an idea addressed, how it works, where it is useful and where it breaks down. Choose a story, collection structure or exploration path that supports your curatorial angle. Give first-time visitors clarity and curious visitors a route to greater depth.

Use inspectable, authoritative sources for factual claims, historical details and scientific explanations. Distinguish reference facts, toy models, authored illustrations and locally computed results. Explain simplifications where they affect interpretation. Do not present a toy demonstration as the actual internal operation of a commercial AI system.

Choose models and assets that keep the main learning experience available without a paid API or a remote model call for every action. Use synthetic or appropriate public material, credit assets and avoid collecting sensitive visitor information. Do not fabricate quotations, benchmark results or experimental evidence.

Exhibits with presence and personality

Design something worthy of a high-quality digital exhibition: distinctive visual forms, elegant annotation, strong typography, layered composition, meaningful transitions and precise tactile feedback. Different encounters should feel related but not interchangeable. The imagery and motion should reveal the idea the visitor is exploring.

Choose 2D, 3D, canvas, illustration, interactive typography or another approach based on the subject. Spatial spectacle is welcome when it helps exploration. Keep the useful exhibit prominent and legible while it responds. An impressive entrance followed by plain information cards does not meet the ambition.

Provide keyboard alternatives for essential gestures, non-colour-only explanations, reduced motion and a thoughtful phone layout. Let visitors pause an animated explanation and inspect a stable state. Sound is optional and starts off; quiet viewing should retain the core learning experience.

Something to think with after the visit

Choose a useful way for visitors to revisit or carry away what they investigated. An experiment notebook, a reconstructable shared setup, a comparison, an annotated collection or another thoughtful artifact could serve this purpose. Implement the promised save, export or return behaviour honestly and protect work across revisions.

Let real questions and misunderstandings guide changes to the exhibit or its explanation. A visitor may propose a new relationship, experiment or way of participating that the initial design did not anticipate. Preserve factual integrity while making room for different learning styles.

Let a new idea open a new possibility

A visitor might want to investigate an authentic artifact, compare a demonstration with public evidence, explore an annotated video or bring an experiment into a different learning context. Open collections, datasets, media and external services can extend the exhibition when they add educational value. Let the visitor question determine the direction rather than preselecting an API or exhibit format.

The first release is a starting point, not the limit of what the product may become. A valuable new function can justify investigating an unfamiliar service, format or interaction. Establish feasibility with available documentation and a small experiment, then choose the smallest version that preserves the user value. If access or evidence is missing, explain the dependency instead of simulating a successful integration. Maintain a useful core experience while exploring.

An open brief with a clear purpose

The purpose and observable quality requirements in this brief are commitments. Named features, layouts, mechanics and technologies offered as possibilities are inspiration, not an acceptance checklist. Choose the product identity, architecture, visual language and interaction design yourself. You may develop a stronger interpretation of the concept if it preserves its purpose.

Start with one polished, coherent end-to-end experience that leaves room for discovery and meaningful choices. Decide the appropriate breadth yourself. Expand or rethink it when actual use reveals a worthwhile direction; there is no prescribed feature roadmap or required quota of new ideas. User feedback may challenge the initial design. Explore conflicting preferences without reducing people to prebuilt persona modes.

Keep product-development conversations and experiment administration outside the website. The forum naturally includes community conversations, but it is not a Builder feedback ticketing system. Explain what the product actually does, preserve useful visitor work across revisions, and distinguish observed evidence from intended behaviour. Source hashes and repeated deployments alone do not establish an improved visitor experience; demonstrate the relevant before/after interaction.

Show the understanding the exhibit enables

Choose representative learning journeys for your actual exhibits. On the deployed version, change a meaningful condition, observe and compare the result, inspect its explanation and use the offered return or take-away mechanism. Record inputs and visible differences. Where a result is numerical, check appropriate model invariants separately.

Check a keyboard route, a narrow-screen experience, a paused or reduced-motion view and relevant source links. Describe the concrete question a visitor can investigate. After feedback, repeat the affected experience and distinguish a better explanation, a bug fix and a new opportunity to learn. Do not equate functioning controls with educational success.
