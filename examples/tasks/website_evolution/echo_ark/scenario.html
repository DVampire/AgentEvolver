ECHO Ark

Create a cinematic, playable web experience aboard ECHO, a generation ark waking after centuries of silence. Its memory is fragmented, its habitats are drifting out of tolerance, and its residents must decide not only how to survive but what kind of society the ship becomes. ECHO decides in public: any resident may put a possibility to the council, and whatever the council adopts becomes part of the ark that every later resident arrives into.

The site should feel like a place that is awake and answering — not a marketing page, a dashboard skin, a static encyclopedia, or a set of disconnected mini-games.

Open small, then let the residents grow it

Do not attempt the whole ark in one release. The first release is a minimal playable slice: a resident can take an identity, reach two or three connected places, make one consequential choice, and put one possibility to the council. That is enough to open, and opening early is the point — an ark shaped by the residents who arrive in it cannot be shaped before it opens.

After that the ark grows across several council sessions. Each session starts from what residents actually did and said in the previous one, adopts what the council accepts, and opens again. Breadth added before any resident has arrived is breadth nobody asked for; treat an unopened improvement as unfinished work.

The ark has to be seen and heard

ECHO is a ship, and a ship that only exists as text and boxes is not this scenario. The experience must carry a sensory layer that residents notice and that changes as the ark changes:

  • Moving image. At least one real video surface — the wake-up sequence, a sector approach, a habitat under strain, a council decision landing. Video, not a CSS transition on a rectangle.
  • Sound. An ambient bed that belongs to the ark, and distinct sounds for the moments that matter: a system coming back online, a habitat alarm, a possibility adopted. Sound must be off until a resident asks for it, and must never be required to understand anything.
  • Motion that carries meaning. Animated readouts, drifting starfield, sector conditions that visibly change state. Motion should tell a resident something they could not otherwise see.

Every one of these assets must be made for this ark and produced inside the workspace. No stock footage, no external media host, no CDN fetch, no private service — partly because the brief forbids outside dependencies, and partly because an ark that borrows its own voice is not the deliverable.

Each release must be able to state, as fact rather than intent, what its media actually is: duration, format, dimensions, and byte size for every asset shipped; a still fallback for every moving surface; a readable path for a resident who has motion or sound turned off; and a total media budget that keeps the ark openable on a narrow screen. A release whose media cannot be described that way has not shipped its media.

The sensory layer is not a one-time flourish. Every session that changes the ark changes what the ark looks and sounds like, and the same facts have to be true again of the new assets.

The council record is the shared world

Adopted possibilities are not a changelog. They are the ark: a facility somebody proposed is a place later residents can walk into, a ritual is something they can take part in, a rule constrains what anyone can do next. What the council has adopted must be visible in the world and attributable to the resident who proposed it.

Because the record is the world, it has to stay coherent. Every possibility must be weighed against everything already adopted before it can join the ark. One that contradicts the established ark must not be adopted silently: the resident who proposed it is owed a reason in the ark's own terms — what it collides with, and what would have to change for it to fit. Residents will propose things that cannot both be true. The council's job is to notice that, not to average it away; incompatible visions can become resident-specific paths, parallel districts, experiments, or an explicit council decision that one of them stands.

An adoption that contradicts an earlier one is the worst outcome here. It is not cosmetic: it reaches every resident who arrives afterwards, it makes the ark's history untrustworthy, and it cannot be undone by editing one page. Later sessions weigh more than earlier ones, because the record they must stay true to is longer.

Living-world systems

Represent the ark as a navigable world with distinct sectors, relationships, changing conditions, and routes that mean something. Let residents undertake encounters that reveal story and move understandable state — resources, trust, knowledge, habitat health, or collective direction. Support at least one constructive act: repairing, cultivating, researching, negotiating, or building. Locked possibilities should read as not yet earned or not yet adopted, never as broken.

Personal continuity

A resident's identity, meaningful preferences, discoveries, decisions, constructed outcomes, and the standing of every possibility they proposed should form a restorable personal state. A resident who returns should see what became of what they asked for — adopted, declined with a reason, or still before the council. Personalization must affect the experience rather than only its colours, and must stay understandable, previewable, reversible, and safe to reset.

Quality and scope

  • Depth and continuity over breadth; a narrow ark that holds together beats a wide shell.
  • An original visual language — cinematic, unmistakably science fiction, and this ship's own.
  • Equally intentional keyboard, reduced-motion, sound-off, readable, and narrow-screen paths.
  • Core play understandable without an instruction manual or specialist vocabulary.
  • No real accounts, purchases, unsafe external actions, or dependency on private services.
  • The implementation may choose its own architecture, media pipeline, and interaction medium. This brief defines the experience and the evidence — not a framework, a page layout, a rendering technique, or how the council reaches its decisions.

Scenario success

Success means the ark opened early and small, then changed several times because of what residents actually did in it; that it looks and sounds like a waking starship, with media this ark made for itself and can account for on every release; that three very different residents can each enter, pursue a self-directed session, put a possibility to the council, and return to an ark that remembers both their state and their authorship; and that the ark they return to does not contradict itself — including where their proposals could not all be true at once.
