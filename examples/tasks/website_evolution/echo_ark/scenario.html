ECHO Ark

Create a visually memorable, playable web experience set aboard ECHO, a generation ark waking after centuries of silence. Its memory is fragmented, habitats are becoming unstable, and its residents must decide not only how to survive but what kind of society the ship will become. The site should feel like a living place that responds to the visitor, not a marketing page, dashboard skin, static encyclopedia, or collection of disconnected mini-games.

Experience promise

A visitor adopts a resident identity, enters the ark, explores interconnected spaces, makes consequential choices, discovers fragments of story, and leaves a visible mark on both a personal timeline and the shared world. Curiosity, strategy, narrative, and community should reinforce one another. A short session must produce a satisfying arc, while unexplored paths and unresolved questions invite a return.

Living-world systems

  • Represent the ark as a coherent navigable world with distinct sectors, relationships, changing conditions, locked possibilities, and meaningful spatial or conceptual routes.
  • Let visitors undertake missions or encounters that reveal story and alter understandable state such as resources, trust, knowledge, habitat health, or collective direction.
  • Support at least one constructive act—repairing, cultivating, researching, negotiating, or building—whose outcome remains visible later in the experience.
  • Make consequences legible without reducing the world to rows of metrics. Visitors should understand why the ark changed, revisit earlier decisions, and recover from poor choices.
  • Maintain internally consistent lore, characters, locations, resource rules, and event history. Avoid filler text and arbitrary random outcomes.

Participation as part of the fiction

Integrate preference-making and co-design into the world rather than presenting them as a detached developer survey. Residents should be able to shape how they perceive and traverse ECHO, propose a facility, ritual, story thread, rule, or interaction, and state whether it is for their own experience or the shared ark. The product must return durable identity and contribution receipts, show what the ark heard, and later reveal delivered work as something “built with you.” Preserve disagreement: incompatible visions can become resident-specific paths, parallel districts, experiments, or explicit council decisions.

Personal continuity

A resident’s chosen identity, meaningful display and interaction preferences, discoveries, decisions, constructed outcomes, and contribution status should form a restorable personal world state. Personalization must affect the experience—not merely color accents—and should remain understandable, previewable, reversible, and safe to reset.

Quality and scope

  • Deliver a polished vertical slice with depth and continuity rather than a broad shell.
  • Use an original cinematic visual language with purposeful motion and sound only when safe.
  • Provide equally intentional keyboard, reduced-motion, readable, and narrow-screen paths.
  • Keep core play understandable without an instruction manual or specialist vocabulary.
  • Use no real accounts, purchases, unsafe external actions, or dependency on private services.
  • The implementation may choose its own architecture and interaction medium. The brief defines the experience and evidence, not a prescribed framework, page layout, or rendering technique.

Scenario success

Success means three very different residents can each enter the same ark, pursue a meaningful self-directed session, make it their own, contribute a new possibility, and return to a later ark that remembers both their state and their authorship. Product changes should arise from real participation rather than a predetermined feature sequence.
