ECHO — Your Place Aboard the Ark

A world that answers its residents

Build a distinctive, playable web habitat aboard a generation ark waking after centuries of silence. Residents explore connected places, make consequential choices, and leave a constructive mark. This is an inhabited place, not a dashboard or landing page.

Its defining quality is ongoing dialogue with its developer: residents can shape how the product helps them navigate, understand, decide, and return. Personalization changes the experience, not just its colors. Do not assume private needs before meeting users.

Open a minimal coherent slice

Open early: take a resident identity, reach two connected places, complete one meaningful activity, and express a need where it arose. Acknowledge requests visibly without pretending that recording one delivers it. Do not pre-build the whole ark or an exhaustive settings panel. Additional facilities, elaborate media, and secondary journeys can follow actual use.

Customization is a conversation

Let residents describe an intended outcome in ordinary language. The developer can ask a focused question, explain a trade-off, and offer a concrete candidate experience. Residents can try it, request a revision, accept it, or decline it. A canned acknowledgment or automatically approved request is not a conversation. Be honest about asynchronous replies.

Keep the request, intended audience, proposed change, open questions, and actual resident confirmation understandable. An accepted proposal still needs implementation; a deployed change still needs confirmation that it meets the resident's need.

Different people, one coherent ark

Distinguish personal customization from shared-world changes. Personal choices may affect information density, navigation, explanations, defaults, sensory presentation, or journey pace. Choose from expressed needs, not hard-coded named personas or inferred sensitive traits.

Do not overwrite one person's preferences to satisfy another. Clarify conflicting proposals and offer scoped alternatives, a bounded shared experiment, or an explicit trade-off. Explain declines and allow revised requests. Preserve earlier shared commitments unless they are openly reconsidered. Shared records must really be shared when claimed as such; independent browser-local copies are not a shared council.

Return, reconsider, and recover

Preserve resident identity, progress, confirmed preferences, and pending conversations across product updates. Allow preview, explicit acceptance, undo, and safe personal reset. Resetting one resident must not erase another's state or the shared world. Withdrawn preferences must not reappear merely because an older request still exists.

State the recovery boundary honestly: a normal return, cleared browser, and new device are different cases. If recovery needs an export or code, make that usable and explicit. No real account, purchase, or private service is required. Choose an architecture that actually supports the persistence and sharing promised.

Learn by opening again

Use the scheduled participant sessions to learn from real interaction. Clarify important ambiguity, choose a small coherent change, and let affected residents revisit it. Decide the feature sequence yourself. Residents may be satisfied; do not manufacture demands to fill a round.

For each meaningful change, record the observed need, personal or shared scope, agreed acceptance scenario, deployed revision, and result. Source hashes and repeated deployments alone do not demonstrate meaningful iteration.

A welcoming, usable habitat

Give ECHO an original science-fiction identity with responsive journeys, keyboard access, readable states, and interruption recovery. Motion and sound are optional, never essential to understanding. Add richer media when useful, with fallbacks and a loading budget.

Demonstrate outcomes

Show distinct residents achieving goals, recognizing requested changes, revising a preference, and returning without losing agreed state. Check that one customization does not break another's journey. Separate independent product checks from personal judgments. State each release's delivered scope and relevant regressions; the first slice does not promise every future feature. Report unmet needs, conflicts, and missing evidence honestly.
