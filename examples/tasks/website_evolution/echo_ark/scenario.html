ECHO — Wake the Ark

A world worth entering

Create a fancy, visually memorable and genuinely playable science-fiction website aboard a generation ship waking after centuries of silence. Its caretaker, ECHO, remembers fragments of the voyage. Visitors discover the world through interaction and leave a recognizable change behind. Aim for the authored atmosphere and tactile polish of a compelling indie-game opening, not a functional prototype with decorative CSS.

The website is the fictional experience, not a developer collaboration portal. Do not turn feedback handling, release approvals or experiment controls into product features. ECHO belongs to the story, not to the development process.

Your design decisions

Choose the ship's spaces, narrative, interaction mechanics, visual language, palette, typography, layout and implementation. There is no required room count, clue count, ending count or exact play time. A puzzle, a creative act, a simulation, an exploration or another coherent approach can work. Requirements describe the experience to deliver; examples are optional inspiration, not a feature checklist.

A short, satisfying experience

Welcome visitors into a compelling scene with a discoverable first action, without an account or long explanation blocking entry. Let them understand enough of the world to act intentionally, then see a meaningful consequence in the world itself. Clicking through prose or changing a counter alone is not the complete experience.

Make interaction responsive and rewarding, with understandable cause and effect. Offer enough agency or discovery to make further exploration or another visit appealing. Help should be available when needed; mistakes should not create unexplained dead ends. Choose a small scope that feels complete in a short visit, and polish it instead of filling the ship with placeholder rooms, menus or nonfunctional controls.

Distinctive, composed and beautiful

Commit to a cohesive art direction: recognizable forms, intentional composition, expressive lighting or color, and carefully chosen typography. Scene artwork and interactive details should feel designed together. A generic card template, a background gradient or decorative effects alone is not enough to establish the setting.

Make the opening visually compelling and keep the next action legible. Adapt the composition deliberately to desktop and narrow mobile screens. Choose imagery and a rendering technique suited to your concept; neither photorealism nor 3D is required. A restrained design can be as striking as an elaborate one when it is well authored.

Use motion and sound where they improve atmosphere or feedback. Interaction stays immediate, sound starts off, and reduced-motion users can still perceive meaningful changes. Fancy means memorable and polished, not visually noisy or slow.

Possible directions, not requirements

A botanical habitat coming alive, an observatory revealing a lost destination, or a musical system restoring the ship's voice could each support this premise. Arrival, discovery and awakening might form an emotional arc, but need not be separate screens or follow a fixed sequence. Use, combine or ignore these ideas. An original direction meeting the experience goals is equally welcome; none of these examples is an acceptance criterion.

A reason to return

Preserve meaningful progress or choices when returning in the same browser with its site storage retained. Provide an understandable way to begin again. Explain the storage boundary briefly, without promising cross-device recovery or shared state unless actually implemented. No real account, payment or private external service is needed.

Later improvements should respond to actual visitor needs. Revisit the initial design when evidence warrants it; do not freeze its mechanics or style just because they shipped first. Different preferences can coexist without forcing every visitor down one path.

Judge the rendered experience

The result should be visually distinctive, enjoyable to interact with and complete enough to demonstrate. A visitor can enter, discover something, act with intention, perceive a consequence, and return to recognizable progress. Evaluate these outcomes against the implemented concept, not optional examples or invented controls.

All visible controls work. Support keyboard navigation, visible focus, readable contrast, reduced motion and use without sound. Keep loading reasonable and provide usable feedback when something fails. Functional correctness does not establish beauty or playability: inspect the rendered scenes and actually try the core interaction. Assess composition, coherence, clarity and responsiveness with concrete evidence, rather than treating a preferred palette or genre as an objective pass condition.
