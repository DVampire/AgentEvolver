The Museum of Unfinished Dreams

A strange place that rewards attention

Build a fancy, atmospheric browser experience that feels like entering a beautifully crafted interactive exhibition. Visitors encounter unfinished artifacts, notice surprising connections, and leave with something they composed themselves. Aim for the polish, tactile feedback and coherence of a small art game, not a gallery grid, portfolio, chatbot, slideshow or endless scroll of poetic text.

Let the exhibition occupy the main view. Choose an original visual grammar: layered illustration, theatrical web composition, an explorable 3D wing or another coherent approach. Depth and atmosphere do not require 3D. Choose the palette, navigation and stack yourself. No developer conversation panel, feedback inbox, approval queue or experiment UI belongs in the museum.

Enter, discover, transform, compose

Offer an inviting threshold and an immediate encounter the visitor can affect. Let them explore connected exhibits in different orders, interact with an artifact, uncover a fragment, and connect it with another in a personal dream atlas. A deliberate choice must visibly change a later reveal, relationship or composition. Clicking to open more text is not enough for every exhibit.

Design a few distinct interactions that belong to the fiction: rearranging an object could reveal a layer, a symbol could resonate with a fragment from another room, or a juxtaposition could change the atlas. These are examples, not prescribed mechanics. Provide readable rules and affordances; mystery must not mean guessing invisible hotspots or waiting for timers.

The atlas should preserve meaningful choices or arrangements, not just completion badges. Let visitors inspect, revise and restore it in the same browser. Different journeys should be capable of leaving different compositions. Welcome interpretation without marking one emotional reading correct or diagnosing the visitor.

A collection with an inner life

Curate a bounded fictional collection with recurring motifs, contrasting voices and relationships worth noticing. Give artifacts specific forms and histories; avoid generic dream quotations, interchangeable glowing orbs and repeated placeholder rooms. Visual and interactive details should carry meaning instead of prose explaining everything. A mystery can remain unresolved if the visit itself feels complete.

Annotations or remixes, if offered, are private local creations by default. Do not pretend they entered a shared exhibition or were reviewed by a curator. Public sharing, collaborative collections or importing personal material are possible later directions, not an initial content pipeline. Use original or appropriately licensed assets and clearly fictional voices. Do not solicit sensitive memories or present invented material as real testimony. The museum is neither therapy nor a dream-analysis service.

One finished wing, not an empty infinite museum

First deliver a small connected exhibition with a complete discover-transform-atlas loop and a satisfying return to the entrance. Offer enough contrast for a second route to matter. Polish these encounters before adding rooms or procedural collections. Make the first interesting action discoverable without an onboarding questionnaire.

The opening must be an interactive composition, not a spectacular loading screen followed by plain cards. Give exhibits distinct silhouettes, materials, typography, light or graphic textures within one art direction. Transitions connect places; interactions respond precisely. Avoid making every room the same dark panel with a new label. Original CSS, SVG, canvas, geometry and licensed imagery are all possibilities; no particular service is required.

Keep wandering comfortable: a clear route back, visible focus, keyboard equivalents for gestures, legible text, a considered phone layout and orientation under reduced motion. Optional audio starts off and meaning remains available without it. Avoid flashes, forced horror, autoplay sound or disorienting travel. Quiet and readable alternatives should preserve the wonder, not replace it with an unrelated transcript.

Room for different imaginations

Preserve discoveries, atlas arrangements and offered preferences across reloads in the same browser, with confirmed reset. Protect saved work when the collection changes and recover explicitly rather than silently discarding it. Explain unavailable storage; do not promise cross-device continuity without implementing it.

Some visitors enjoy ambiguity, others understandable mechanisms, others making something. Let their encounters and conversations influence later versions. Adaptation may change an encounter or a way of composing, not merely a colour setting. Accommodate conflicting tastes without prebuilt persona modes or a fixed roadmap. Product dialogue happens outside the exhibit; it is not a request for visitor-to-developer messaging inside the museum.

Experience the exhibit, not just its markup

Use the exact deployed version to enter, affect an artifact, reveal a fragment, make a choice that changes a later encounter, compose and revise an atlas, return and reload it. Check a keyboard route, phone layout and reduced motion. Console-injected discoveries do not count as a visit. If a prerequisite fails, mark dependent checks blocked and address it before repeating the journey.

Show observed before/after states and differences between choices. Separately describe visual craft and the reason to explore another route; functioning buttons alone do not establish atmosphere or agency. Compare equivalent views across revisions, distinguishing cosmetic changes, bug fixes and new experiences. State untested claims honestly.
