The Museum of Unfinished Dreams

Create an atmospheric digital museum where visitors do more than browse exhibits: they wander through dreamlike rooms, interpret incomplete artifacts, assemble personal constellations of meaning, and contribute fragments that can transform future exhibitions. It should feel like a poetic place with discoverable rules, not a conventional gallery grid, portfolio, or chatbot.

Core journey

A visitor crosses a threshold, chooses how they wish to be guided, explores several connected exhibits, notices relationships that are not immediately explained, and leaves with a personal dream atlas. Encounters should reward attention and interpretation through changing text, sound-safe visual composition, objects, memories, spatial transitions, or other native web interactions. At least one choice should alter what is revealed later.

Museum systems

  • Curate a coherent collection with recurring symbols, histories, voices, and mysteries.
  • Allow nonlinear discovery while preserving orientation and a recoverable path.
  • Let visitors collect, connect, annotate, remix, or reinterpret selected fragments.
  • Make the personal atlas restorable and meaningfully different for different choices.
  • Give collective contributions a thoughtful lifecycle: received, considered, exhibited, transformed, deferred, or declined, with provenance rather than anonymous content sludge.

Co-curation

Make visitor participation part of the museum fiction. A visitor may propose an exhibit, missing perspective, sensory mode, curatorial rule, connection between artifacts, or new way to encounter the collection. They must be able to protect something non-negotiable, choose personal/shared/speculative scope, and describe the moment that would prove their idea works. Later releases should credit delivered ideas as co-curated without exposing private material.

Design values

Balance ambiguity with agency: mysterious is not the same as confusing. Respect reduced motion, keyboard navigation, readable modes, narrow screens, content warnings, quiet modes, and graceful recovery. Avoid appropriating real trauma or presenting generated material as a real person’s memory. Prefer a deeply finished exhibition wing over many empty rooms. The implementation controls its stack and visual grammar; this brief does not prescribe pages.

Scenario success

Success means visitors with contrasting tastes can experience distinct yet coherent museum journeys, recognize their own curatorial agency, return to their saved atlas, and see how a later museum learned from their authorship. Evidence—not a predetermined evolution quota—must decide whether new reusable Builder capabilities are created.
